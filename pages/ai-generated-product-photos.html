AI-Generated Product Photos: How to Check Before You Buy

AI tools have made it cheap and easy to generate photorealistic product images without ever owning the actual product. Sellers on Amazon, Etsy, eBay, and AliExpress use them to make cheap items look premium — or to list products that don't exist at all. Here's how to spot them in under 30 seconds before you order.

Why sellers use AI-generated product photos

Professional product photography costs $50-$500 per image when done by a photographer. AI image generation tools produce a photorealistic product shot in under a minute for pennies. For a dropshipping seller who may never handle the physical product, AI-generated images are dramatically more accessible — and tempting to abuse.

There are three categories of AI product photo use, ranging from benign to fraudulent:

Benign: AI enhancement of real product photos

Background removal, lighting correction, color grading applied to real photographs of the actual product. The product is real and the image is representative; AI just cleaned up the photo. Many legitimate sellers use this.

Grey area: AI lifestyle photos around a real product

A real product placed in a completely AI-generated environment — a kitchen background, a model's hands, an outdoor setting that wasn't in the original shoot. The product is real but the context is fictional. This can misrepresent size, finish, or quality in context.

Fraudulent: AI images of a non-existent or misrepresented product

The product shown in the images doesn't match the item that ships, or the product doesn't exist at all. AI generates an image of a luxury item; the customer receives a cheap knockoff or nothing. This violates FTC deceptive advertising guidelines and platform terms of service.

Visual red flags in AI product photos

Some tells are visible without a detector, especially once you know what to look for:

Too-perfect surfaces

Real products have micro-scratches, texture variation, and manufacturing marks. AI-generated surfaces are often unnaturally uniform — leather looks like it was painted, wood grain is too even, metal has no real reflections.

Impossible studio backgrounds

Backgrounds that are perfectly seamless, gradient from nothing, or have no floor-wall seam. Real product photos, even professional ones, have physical constraints. AI can place products in physically impossible lighting environments.

Wrong shadows or reflections

AI generators frequently produce shadows that don't match the direction or color temperature of the apparent light source, or reflections in product surfaces that show scenes inconsistent with the surroundings.

Sizing that looks off

Without a real reference object (a hand, a standard-sized item, a flat surface), AI images can make any product look any size. A bag that looks large in the listing is a common deception for small fashion accessories.

Text or logos that look blurred or wrong

If the product has printed text, labels, or logos, AI often renders them incorrectly — letters blurred, fonts inconsistent, or brand names garbled. Real product photos have legible text.

Only one angle

A real seller who actually has the product can photograph it from multiple angles. An AI-generated listing often has one or two hero images, no close-ups of construction or material, and no photos of the packaging or accessories.

Which platforms are most affected?

AI product photos are a problem across all major marketplaces, but the risk level varies by platform's seller verification and quality controls:

Platform Risk level Notes
AliExpress / Temu / Shein High Low barriers to selling; many dropshippers who've never handled the product
Facebook Marketplace / Instagram Shops High Minimal seller verification; new accounts easy to create
eBay (new or low-feedback sellers) Medium-High Established sellers with feedback history are lower risk; new sellers warrant scrutiny
Etsy Medium Handmade policy means AI-generated items technically violate terms; enforcement inconsistent
Amazon (third-party sellers) Medium Amazon's image accuracy requirements are strict; enforcement lags on new listings
Amazon (sold by Amazon / verified brands) Low Brand registry and direct fulfillment provide more accountability

Common questions

How can I tell if a product photo is AI-generated?

Right-click the image in Chrome and use FauxSpy to run an instant AI detection check. Visual tells include: backgrounds that look impossibly clean, product surfaces with no real-world wear, shadows inconsistent with the light source, and text or logos that appear blurred or garbled. Run a reverse image search too — if the same image appears across multiple listings or stock photo sites, it isn't a photo of the seller's actual product.

Is using AI-generated photos in product listings illegal?

Using AI to generate photos of a product that looks significantly different from the actual item, or listing products that don't exist, can violate FTC deceptive advertising guidelines. Amazon's image policy explicitly requires that main images accurately represent the actual product. AI enhancement of real photos (background removal, lighting) is generally permitted. The legality depends on whether the AI image misrepresents what the buyer will receive.

Do Amazon and Etsy allow AI-generated product photos?

Neither platform explicitly bans AI-generated images, but both require listing images to accurately represent the actual product. Amazon's image requirements are strict and violations can result in listing removal or account suspension. Etsy's handmade seller policy creates complications for AI-generated items (not just images). AI-generated lifestyle backgrounds around a real product exist in a grey area; AI images that misrepresent a product clearly violate both platforms' terms.

What are the red flags for fake product listings beyond the photos?

Prices significantly below comparable legitimate sellers; seller accounts with no history or very recent creation; reviews clustered in a short time window; product descriptions with generic or poorly-translated language; no clear return policy; shipping times of 4-6 weeks from overseas for items that appear domestic. Combine AI photo detection with these listing-level signals for better protection.
