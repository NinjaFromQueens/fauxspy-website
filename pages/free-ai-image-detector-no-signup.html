Why do most AI image detectors require sign-up even for a free tier?

The most common reason is rate-limit enforcement. Without an account, a tool can't reliably cap usage per person — anyone can just reload a page or clear cookies to reset their count. Account creation lets tools enforce daily limits accurately and collect email addresses for marketing.

The second reason is product qualification: account creation is a proxy for intent. Users who create an account are more likely to eventually upgrade to paid, making them worth tracking in a CRM. Anonymous free users are harder to convert.

FauxSpy enforces its 10/day limit through the extension itself using local browser storage — no server-side account needed. BitMind uses in-memory analysis with zero data retention, which is why it can operate with no account at all. Both approaches work for casual use; neither scales to the "unlimited daily checks" use case, which requires Pro.

What to look for beyond the free limit

The daily free limit matters, but it's not the only relevant factor. Three other things worth checking before picking a free-tier tool:

Does it work in your browser, or do you have to upload?

Upload-based tools (AI or Not, Illuminarty's web UI) require you to save the image first, then navigate to the site, then upload — three steps. Extension-based tools that work directly on the page (FauxSpy, BitMind, Is It AI?, WasItAI, UncovAI) let you right-click or hover without leaving the site you're on. If you're checking photos while browsing dating profiles or social media, in-page scanning is significantly faster.

Does it tell you WHY it flagged something?

Most tools return a binary verdict or a confidence percentage. Illuminarty is the notable exception — it overlays a heatmap on the image showing exactly which regions triggered the AI verdict, which helps you understand whether the flag is due to a specific face, background, or texture. For users who want to understand the reasoning, not just the verdict, Illuminarty's free tier (≈5/day) is worth using alongside a higher-volume tool.

What does it do when it's not sure?

Some tools force a binary verdict even when confidence is low. FauxSpy returns Inconclusive when confidence falls below its threshold — the conservative approach reduces false positives at the cost of some uncategorized results. If you're checking photos of real people who'd be harmed by a wrong "definitely AI" verdict (a real person being accused of using a fake photo), the Inconclusive category is actually more useful than a forced answer.

Is It AI?'s Chrome extension has a polished feature set: side-panel results that stay open while you browse, generation-method identification (it attempts to identify whether an image was made with Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, etc.), NSFW scoring, and accessibility features. Is the free tier accurate enough?

For most consumer use cases — checking a dating profile photo, a suspicious marketplace listing, or a news image — yes. Accuracy benchmarks for the leading tools in 2026 cluster in the 88-91% range on standard test sets of AI-generated images. FauxSpy uses Sightengine's genai API, which benchmarks at approximately 88% accuracy with sub-second processing on photorealistic AI images.

One important caveat: accuracy figures assume the tool is being tested against images from known AI generators running their default settings. Against images that have been specifically optimized to evade AI detectors — run through multiple post-processing steps, fine-tuned to fool a specific detector — Imagera's 2026 research found that no detector tested exceeded 45-60% accuracy. This is not a reason to avoid using a detector; it's a reason to treat any result as one signal in a verification process, not a definitive verdict.

For high-stakes verification — journalism, legal proceedings, identity disputes — a negative result from any free-tier tool should be treated as "not confirmed AI" rather than "confirmed real." Use a detector result alongside reverse image search and contextual assessment, not instead of them.
