The catfishing problem nobody talks about

Stolen photos used to be the go-to. Now you don't need a real person at all. A convincing AI-generated face takes about 10 seconds to produce and costs nothing. And because they look so polished — perfect skin, symmetrical features, that soft uncanny quality around the eyes — they actually fool people more reliably than stolen real photos do.

Americans reported $672 million in romance and confidence-scam losses to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center in 2024. A lot of that starts with a profile photo that was never real.

How do you check if a dating profile photo is AI-generated?

  1. Install Faux Spy (free, 3 checks/day)

  4. Click it. You'll get a verdict and confidence score in a few seconds

That's it. No copying URLs, no uploading images, no separate website to visit.

What do the results mean?

AI Photo — the model detected a photorealistic AI-generated image. The higher the confidence percentage, the stronger the signal. Above 80% is worth taking seriously.

Real Photo — looks like an authentic photograph. Confidence matters here too: 95% "Real" is very different from 55% "Real."

Inconclusive — not enough signal to commit either way. This happens most often with small, heavily compressed thumbnails. If you can get to a full-size version of the photo, try that instead.

Does it work on the app itself?

Yes — if you're using the browser version. Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge all have full-featured web apps you can open in Chrome. Faux Spy works on any website, so if you can see the photo in your browser, you can scan it.

The native iOS and Android apps can't run browser extensions, so you'd need to switch to the web version for those photos. For most use cases, that's a quick switch worth making.

What red flags should you check besides the photo?

A photo check tells you whether the face is real. It doesn't tell you whether the person behind it is honest. These behaviour patterns show up in most romance scams, whatever the photo looks like:

  • They avoid live video. A camera that's always "broken" is the single most common tell. A real person can do a 30-second video call.
  • They push you off the app fast. Moving to WhatsApp, Telegram or text within a day or two takes you away from the dating app's moderation and reporting tools.
  • The relationship moves unusually fast. Talk of love, a future together or "fate" within days, before you've met.
  • Their story doesn't add up. Details about their job, location or family shift over time, or they're conveniently stationed abroad, on an oil rig, or travelling for work.
  • Money comes up. A sudden emergency, a customs fee, a ticket to visit you, or a "can't-lose" crypto investment. Anyone you haven't met in person who asks for money should be treated as a scammer.

AI detector or reverse image search: which do you need?

Both. They catch different kinds of fake, and a scammer can use either one.

What should you do if a match's photo comes back as AI?

  1. Don't send money or personal details — not for an emergency, a visit, or an investment.
  2. Ask for a short live video call. A real person can say yes. A scammer will have an excuse.
  3. Report the profile in the app. Tinder, Bumble and Hinge all have a report option on every profile, and reports help them remove networks of fake accounts.
  4. If you've lost money, report it. In the U.S., file with the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and the FBI at ic3.gov, and contact your bank right away.

A high-confidence "AI Photo" result on its own is a strong warning, not proof of fraud — some people use AI-edited photos of themselves. The behaviour above is what tells you whether it's a scam.

Common questions

Which dating apps does it work on?

Any dating app with a browser version: Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, OkCupid, Match, Plenty of Fish, Coffee Meets Bagel. If the photo loads in Chrome, Faux Spy can scan it.

Can it detect photos stolen from real people's accounts?

No — Faux Spy detects AI-generated images specifically, not whether a photo was stolen from someone else's profile. For that, Google Images or TinEye reverse image search is what you want. The two tools work well together: run both for a thorough check.

What if the result is wrong?

No detector is perfect — that's why Faux Spy gives you a confidence score instead of a flat verdict. Treat it as a strong signal, not a final word. A 92% "AI Photo" is compelling. A 58% one is worth noting but isn't decisive on its own.
