SynthID Checker: How to Check an Image for an AI Watermark

SynthID is an invisible watermark that Google, OpenAI and a few other companies put in the images, videos and audio their AI makes. Here's how to check for it, what the result tells you, and what to do when there's no watermark to find.

Quick Answer

To check an image for SynthID, upload it to Google's SynthID Detector at synthid.com, or to the Gemini app and ask "Was this created with Google AI?" If a watermark is found, the image was made or edited with AI from Google or a partner such as OpenAI. If none is found, that doesn't mean the image is real: many AI tools don't add SynthID, and watermarks can be removed. For those images, use an AI image detector that looks at the pixels.

What is SynthID?

SynthID is a watermark made by Google DeepMind. It's hidden in the content itself, so you can't see it in an image or hear it in audio, and deleting a file's metadata doesn't remove it.

Google adds it to content from its own AI tools, such as Gemini and Veo. Google says it has watermarked more than 180 billion images and videos so far. Other companies use it too: Google names OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao as partners whose watermarks its detector can read, and has announced Apple as a partner.

SynthID is different from C2PA Content Credentials. Content Credentials are a signed label stored in the file's metadata, saying which tool made or edited it. That label is easy to lose when an image is screenshotted or re-saved. SynthID is built into the pixels, so it's meant to survive more of that.

How to check an image or video for SynthID

Only Google's own tools can read SynthID. There are two ways to use them:

  1. Google's SynthID Detector (synthid.com). Google opened it to everyone on October 7, 2026, worldwide and in English. Upload an image, video or audio file and it tells you whether it found a watermark from Google or one of its partners. Upload the best-quality copy you have: a crisp original gives the clearest answer.
  2. The Gemini app. Upload the image and ask "Was this created with Google AI?" or "Is this AI-generated?" Gemini checks for SynthID and explains what it found. Google says Gemini can also check video and audio, and is adding checks for C2PA Content Credentials.

Some other websites call themselves "SynthID checkers". To be sure you're using Google's detector, check that the address is synthid.com.

What the result means

  • Watermark found: the content was made or edited with AI from Google or a partner. It doesn't tell you how much: a fully generated picture and a real photo touched up with AI can give the same answer.
  • No watermark found: this is not proof the image is real. Google's detector says so itself: it is "not a general AI detector." Plenty of AI images will never carry SynthID, including those from companies that haven't adopted it and from open models people run on their own computers.

Watermarks can also be stripped. Google says no signal is completely robust, and researchers presenting at USENIX Security 2026 reported attacks that removed SynthID from images while keeping them looking the same.

No watermark? Check the pixels instead

That's where an AI image detector comes in. Instead of looking for a watermark, it looks at the image itself for the patterns AI generators leave behind. That works on images from any AI tool, whether or not they were watermarked.

Common questions

How do I check an image for SynthID?

Upload it to Google's SynthID Detector at synthid.com, which Google opened to everyone on October 7, 2026. You can also upload it to the Gemini app and ask "Was this created with Google AI?" Both check for the invisible SynthID watermark.

If no SynthID watermark is found, is the image real?

No. SynthID only shows up in content from Google and the companies that have adopted it, such as OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao. Images from other AI tools have no SynthID watermark at all, and watermarks can be removed. Google's own detector says it is not a general AI detector. To check those images, use an AI image detector that looks at the pixels.

Can Faux Spy detect SynthID?

No. Only Google's tools can read SynthID. Faux Spy checks the image itself for the patterns AI generators leave, so it works on images from any AI tool, with or without a watermark. The two checks work well together.

Do ChatGPT images have SynthID?

OpenAI is one of the companies Google names as bringing SynthID to its AI-generated content, and Google's SynthID Detector recognizes OpenAI's watermark. An older ChatGPT image, or one that has been heavily edited, may not carry it.

Can a SynthID watermark be removed?

Sometimes. Google says no signal is completely robust, and researchers presenting at USENIX Security 2026 reported attacks that removed SynthID from images while keeping them looking the same. A missing watermark is never proof that an image is real.
