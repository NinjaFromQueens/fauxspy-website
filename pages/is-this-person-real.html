Is This Person Real? How to Check Before You Trust Them

A match, a new follower, a recruiter, a seller: you've only seen their photos. Here's how to check whether the person behind the profile is real, in a few minutes and for free.

Two ways a profile can be fake

A fake profile usually uses one of two kinds of photo, and each needs a different check:

  • A face made with AI. The person doesn't exist. AI tools can make a realistic face in seconds, so there's no original photo anywhere online. A reverse image search finds nothing, which can make the profile look more trustworthy, not less. What AI faces look like.
  • Stolen photos of a real person. The face is real but belongs to someone else, often taken from their social media. An AI detector will say the photo is real, because it is. A reverse image search is what catches these.

How to check if someone is real, step by step

  1. Check the photo for AI. Screenshot their profile photo and upload it to Faux Spy's free checker, or paste the image's link. You'll get a verdict with a confidence score in about two seconds. On a computer, the browser extension lets you right-click any photo instead. A high AI score means the face probably doesn't exist. Screenshots work, but the original photo gives a clearer result. Why real photos are sometimes flagged.
  2. Run a reverse image search. Upload the same photo to Google Lens, Bing or TinEye. If it shows up under a different name, on a stock photo site, or on someone else's account, the photos are stolen. The FTC's own advice for spotting romance scams is to "do a reverse image search of the person's profile picture." Reverse image search vs. AI detector.
  3. Look at all their photos, not just one. Fake profiles often have only a few photos, all similarly polished, with no candid shots and no pictures with friends. Check each one: a scammer may mix an AI face with stolen photos.
  4. Look at the account itself. A brand-new account, very few friends or connections, a vague bio, or details that don't add up (where they live, what they do, how old they are) are all reasons to slow down.
  5. Ask for a live video call. A real person can usually manage a quick video call. Ask them to do something simple on the spot, like wave or turn their head. Video can be faked with deepfake tools, but a live, unscripted call is much harder to fake than a photo. Constant excuses not to call are a warning sign.
  6. Watch what they ask for. According to the FTC, romance scammers say they can't meet in person and eventually ask for money, often by wire transfer, gift cards, payment apps or cryptocurrency. The FTC's rule is simple: "Never send money or gifts to a sweetheart you haven't met in person."

Signs a face might be made with AI

Newer AI tools make fewer mistakes, so a clean-looking photo isn't proof that someone is real. Still, look closely at:

  • Ears, teeth and jewelry: earrings that don't match, too many teeth, ears that melt into hair.
  • Backgrounds: blurry shapes, bent lines, text that turns into gibberish.
  • Skin and lighting: skin that looks airbrushed, light that doesn't match the background.
  • Every photo looking like a professional portrait, even the "casual" ones.

Because these signs are getting harder to spot by eye, an AI image checker is the faster and more reliable test. More examples: how to spot AI-generated images.

If you think the person isn't real

  • Stop sending money or personal details, even if they push or give you a deadline.
  • Talk to someone you trust, as the FTC recommends. A friend or family member can often see warning signs that are hard to notice from the inside.
  • Report the profile to the app or site where you met them.
  • Report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, especially if you've lost money.

Remember that a photo check is one signal. A real photo doesn't make someone honest, and a high AI score isn't proof on its own. Put the checks together before you decide.

Common questions

How can I tell if a person online is real?

Check their profile photo with an AI image detector to catch faces made with AI, and do a reverse image search to catch photos stolen from someone else. Then ask for a live video call, and be wary of anyone who can't meet, avoids video or asks for money. No single check is proof, so use several.

Can AI make a photo of a person who doesn't exist?

Yes. AI image tools can make a realistic photo of a face that belongs to no one, in a few seconds. Because there's no original, a reverse image search won't find it. An AI image detector, which looks for the patterns AI leaves in the pixels, is the right check for these.

Is a reverse image search enough?

No. A reverse image search finds real photos that were stolen from someone else, but it finds nothing for a face made with AI, because that face has never been online before. Use it together with an AI image detector.

Can someone fake a video call?

It's possible with deepfake tools, but a live, unscripted video call is still much harder to fake than a photo. Ask them to do something simple on the spot, like wave or turn their head. Someone who always has a reason not to video call is a warning sign in itself.
